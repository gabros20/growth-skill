## The AARRR split, reciprocated

marketing's own shipped note states the split from its side: *"AARRR spans three packs:
`marketing` owns acquisition, `growth` owns the experiments that improve conversion across the
funnel, `success` owns retention"* (marketing-skill `SKILL.md`). Reciprocated from growth's side:

- **`marketing` owns Acquisition** — the channels, positioning, and demand that bring someone to
  the door. Growth never creates demand; it evaluates the experiments marketing's hypotheses
  generate.
- **`growth` owns the experiments that improve conversion across the whole funnel** — Activation,
  Referral loop design and math, and Retention *experimentation* (does a resurrection nudge
  actually work). This is why the job table above starts at Activation, not Acquisition.
- **`success` owns Retention *execution*** — the onboarding education and retention
  communications a growth test showed were worth sending. Growth designs and reads out the test;
  `success` sends the message the test validated. See [handoff.md](handoff.md) for the line
  stated as a provisional seam, the same way marketing drew its own `sales`/`success` lines.

A practitioner who expects Acquisition and Retention-execution inside this pack is reading AARRR
as one undivided funnel. This family reads it as five letters distributed to whoever actually owns
the decision at each one — not a gap left by this pack, a seam drawn on purpose.

## A vocabulary trap: "loops" means two things

The incumbent ecosystem uses "loops" for two unrelated concepts, and conflating them produces
wrong advice. One widely-cloned pack's `marketing-loops` skill names *operational, recurring agent
workflows on a cadence* — a weekly SEO scan, an ad-fatigue check — which is `automation`'s
territory, structurally unrelated to growth. A **growth loop**, as used throughout this pack and
in the mental-model table above, is *structurally compounding*: the output of one cycle becomes
the input to the next (an invited user becomes an inviter; a piece of user-generated content
becomes another user's acquisition surface). Same word, two different concepts — see
[referral-and-product-loops.md](referral-and-product-loops.md) for the full disambiguation applied
to a specific referral design.

## Loop math the ecosystem never built

Growth-loop content in the incumbent skill ecosystem is taxonomy, not math: one widely-cloned pack
names five loop types with no K-factor, no cycle time, no compounding arithmetic attached to any
of them, and no attribution to the lineage in the table above. That gap — confirmed by grepping
every growth-adjacent pack cloned for this build — is this section's whitespace to fill. It stops
at the loop's own arithmetic; a live cohort's flatten-or-die health and its retention-curve shape
belong to [funnel-and-cohort-diagnosis.md](funnel-and-cohort-diagnosis.md) and
[retention-and-resurrection.md](retention-and-resurrection.md), not here.

**K-factor.** K = i × c, where i is the number of invites (or shares, or referrals) an existing
active user sends per period, and c is the conversion rate of an invite into a new active user.
K ≥ 1 means the loop is, in principle, self-sustaining — each cohort produces at least one full
replacement cohort with no new paid or organic input. K < 1 means the loop decays and needs an
external input (spend, content, sales) to keep growing. This is a standard convention within the
loops-vs-funnels lineage above, not one practitioner's finding — do not cite a specific company's
K-factor as a benchmark; none was independently verified for this pack, and an unsourced K-factor
number is exactly the kind of folklore figure this family's provenance discipline exists to catch.

**Cycle time.** The wall-clock time for one full pass through the loop — invite sent, new user
activated, new user reaches the point where they can invite in turn. Two loops with identical K
compound at very different real-world rates if their cycle times differ by an order of
magnitude — a daily loop and a monthly loop with the same K are not the same growth engine. Cycle
time, not K alone, decides how fast a viable loop actually compounds in calendar time.

**Compounding arithmetic, and what it hides.** After n cycles, a loop with constant K compounds
approximately as N₀ × Kⁿ new users from the loop alone. Two things this simple form hides, and
that any real read must check before trusting it: K is rarely constant — it typically decays as
the addressable network saturates, so an early K-factor is not the loop's K-factor at scale — and
"new users from the loop" is not the same population as new users overall unless the loop is the
only acquisition channel; blending the two overstates the loop's real contribution. Treat any
reported steady-state K the same way this family treats a benchmark: it needs source, sample,
method, date, and caveat before it travels anywhere.

## Validation

- Every attributed concept in the mental-model table carries a named source and a date, or is
  marked unverified — no concept is asserted without one.
- The Racecar framework is never attributed to Balfour.
- Loops-vs-funnels (measurement) and loops-vs-moats (defensibility) are kept as two distinct
  claims, never merged into one.
- No specific company's K-factor, cycle time, or compounding figure is presented as a verified
  anchor — the formulas are taught; numbers are not invented to illustrate them.
- The AARRR split stated here matches marketing's shipped note in substance, not just in the words
  "Acquisition" and "Retention."
- "Loops" is disambiguated, or the reader is pointed to the disambiguation, before the word is
  used unqualified anywhere downstream.

## Failure modes and handoff

- Citing the Racecar framework to Balfour or "the Balfour/Reforge school" — the corrected,
  primary-sourced attribution is Hockenmaier + Rachitsky.
- Treating loops-over-moats (a defensibility argument) as if it were the loops-vs-funnels
  measurement claim — they are related and often co-cited, never identical.
- Reporting a specific company's K-factor as a verified benchmark when this pack's corpus never
  independently confirmed one.
- Citing Verna's current feed as if it still carries her 2021–22 framework-dense positions.
- Restating [referral-and-product-loops.md](referral-and-product-loops.md)'s own "loops"
  disambiguation or [funnel-and-cohort-diagnosis.md](funnel-and-cohort-diagnosis.md)'s 3-way
  retention definition here instead of pointing to them — this file owns the vocabulary and the
  loop-level math, not the diagnostic mechanics built on top of it.
- Reading the absence of `acquisition` and `retention-execution` jobs in this pack as an
  oversight instead of the AARRR seam stated above — see [handoff.md](handoff.md) for the full
  seam map to marketing and success.
